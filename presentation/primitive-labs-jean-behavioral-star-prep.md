# Primitive Labs: Behavioral Interview STAR Story Bank

Interview: Jean Farmer, Co-Founder and CTO  
Role: Designer  
Focus: product judgment, AI systems, engineering partnership, ownership, learning, and craft

## The Throughline

The interviewer should leave with one clear impression:

> Yankun understands complex systems, finds the real user problem, and works with technical partners to turn uncertainty into a clear product direction.

Reinforce these signals across the conversation:

- Systems thinking without unnecessary complexity.
- Strong interaction and visual judgment.
- Comfort with technical constraints and uncertain AI behavior.
- Direct, low-ego collaboration with product and engineering.
- Initiative from problem framing through outcome.
- Honest separation between shipped work and directional prototypes.

## How To Use STAR Without Sounding Rehearsed

Do not announce “Situation, Task, Action, Result.” Use the logic naturally.

1. **Answer first:** Give your position in one sentence.
2. **Situation:** Explain only the context needed to understand the stakes.
3. **Task:** Name the decision or problem you personally owned.
4. **Action:** Describe two or three important decisions you made.
5. **Result:** Give the measured outcome, product decision, or honest learning.
6. **Reflection:** End with the principle you now carry forward.

Aim for 45 to 75 seconds. A useful answer shape is:

> Claim → tension → decision → result → lesson

Avoid process inventories such as “first I researched, then I wireframed, then I prototyped.” Name what the evidence changed.

## Primary Story 1: CHAI Adoption Turnaround

**Best for:** ambiguity, ownership, product judgment, customer focus, influence, and measurable impact.

**First-principles point:** A technically capable product has no value if people must leave their real work to discover how to use it.

### STAR

- **Situation:** CHAI could answer administrator questions, but monthly adoption was around 3%.
- **Task:** Determine whether the problem was the chat experience itself or CHAI's place in the administrator workflow.
- **Action:** Studied what administrators were actually trying to accomplish and found three recurring jobs: find information, understand data, and fix issues. Reframed CHAI from a separate destination into contextual help inside search, reports, dashboards, workspaces, and devices. Brought product, research, engineering, data partners, and technical experts into the new model.
- **Result:** CHAI's monthly adoption grew from 3% to 18%. In a separate but related search initiative, Smart Search reduced dead-end searches by 86%.
- **Reflection:** Technical capability is not the same as customer value. The product must meet people where the intent and context already exist.

### Answer To Say

- “A strong example is CHAI. It could answer questions, but monthly adoption was only around 3%.”
- “The obvious response was to improve the chat interface, but I wanted to understand whether chat was the real problem.”
- “Research showed that administrators were not trying to become better prompt writers. They were trying to find information, understand data, and fix issues.”
- “I helped move CHAI into those workflows through contextual entry points in search, reports, dashboards, workspaces, and devices.”
- “Monthly adoption grew from 3% to 18%. The lesson was that we improved the outcome by changing where AI created value, not only by polishing the chat surface.”

### If They Push

- **What did you personally own?** “I framed the workflow problem, defined the contextual interaction model, designed the key experiences, and used research and prototypes to align the broader team.”
- **How do you know the change worked?** “The broader CHAI program measured monthly adoption moving from 3% to 18%. I keep that separate from the 86% Smart Search result.”
- **What would you do differently?** “I would instrument the discovery-to-task funnel earlier so we could see exactly which entry points and jobs created repeat use.”

## Primary Story 2: Agentic React Prototype

**Best for:** ambiguity, initiative, speed, AI fluency, prototyping, and cross-functional alignment.

**First-principles point:** If trust depends on timing, state, and handoffs, static screens cannot answer the most important questions.

### STAR

- **Situation:** The organization wanted to explore agentic AI in Control Hub, but “agentic” could mean anything from a better assistant to autonomous administration.
- **Task:** Turn the strategy into a product model that product, design, and engineering could inspect and challenge.
- **Action:** Defined the workflow as intent, context, plan, permission, execution, recovery, and Activity. Built a functional React prototype using AI coding tools. Included approval, progress, interruption, error, and record states instead of demonstrating only a happy path.
- **Result:** The prototype gave stakeholders a concrete direction to evaluate and made feedback more specific, especially around user control and system accountability.
- **Reflection:** A prototype is valuable when it exposes uncertainty and improves a decision, not merely when it looks finished.

### Answer To Say

- “The Agentic project began as a broad strategy rather than a clear product brief.”
- “I first defined the user contract: what the agent knows, what it plans to do, what requires approval, and what is recorded afterward.”
- “Static screens hid the difficult questions around timing, execution, interruption, and failure, so I built the behavior in React.”
- “That let product and engineering experience the handoffs and challenge the model with much more precision.”
- “The work was directional, not a shipped metrics story, but it turned an abstract strategy into a product direction the team could evaluate.”

### If They Push

- **Why code it?** “The uncertainty was in behavior and state, not visual layout. A working prototype was the fastest way to test that.”
- **What did AI tools do?** “They accelerated implementation. I still owned the workflow, constraints, acceptance criteria, and final review.”
- **What was the biggest learning?** “The plan is not hidden model reasoning. It is the contract the administrator is being asked to approve.”

## Primary Story 3: `@Add Context` To Skills

**Best for:** disagreement with engineering, customer evidence, resource awareness, influence, and interaction design.

**First-principles point:** Users should choose a capability in their own language, not operate the AI system's internal routing model.

### STAR

- **Situation:** An early CHAI design asked users to `@Add context` so the system would know which product area or data source to use. Engineering had already started implementing it.
- **Task:** Correct a confusing interaction without ignoring the team's existing investment and limited capacity.
- **Action:** Spoke with users and confirmed that “context” did not explain what CHAI could actually help them do. Reframed the model as Skills described in administrator language. Reused existing components where possible to reduce rework.
- **Result:** Engineering agreed to the revised direction because the customer evidence was clear and the new solution respected the implementation constraint.
- **Reflection:** I do not try to win disagreements by repeating a design opinion. I make the user problem concrete and change my own proposal when needed.

### Answer To Say

- “One disagreement happened after engineering had already implemented an `@Add context` interaction for CHAI.”
- “I found that users did not understand what ‘context’ meant or what choosing it would let CHAI do.”
- “Engineering reasonably pushed back because the team had already invested time and had limited capacity for rework.”
- “I brought back customer evidence and redesigned the model as clear, user-facing Skills while reusing as much of the implementation as possible.”
- “The team agreed to the change because the evidence was stronger and the revised design respected the cost of changing direction.”

### If They Push

- **Who was right?** “Engineering was right to protect the implementation cost. I was right that the user model needed to change. The final answer respected both.”
- **What did you learn?** “Raise uncertain language and mental models before implementation whenever possible, but do not defend sunk work when customer evidence shows the model is wrong.”

## Primary Story 4: Agent Activity And The Existing Audit Log

**Best for:** disagreement with product, compromise, platform thinking, trust, and low-ego leadership.

**First-principles point:** Protect the user need underneath the proposed screen. The right surface may change while the requirement remains.

### STAR

- **Situation:** Agent actions needed a clear record of plans, approvals, touched resources, execution, and outcomes. The first direction proposed a dedicated Activity experience.
- **Task:** Preserve accountability without creating a second source of truth inside Control Hub.
- **Action:** The PM argued that the existing audit log should remain authoritative. Agreed that a parallel audit system would fragment the platform, but showed that existing events lacked the detail required for agent actions. Worked with engineering to identify what could be captured and proposed richer agent-event details inside the existing audit system.
- **Result:** The direction preserved platform coherence while strengthening the transparency required for agent actions. Both sides changed their original position.
- **Reflection:** A strong disagreement should improve the shared product model, not produce a winner and loser.

### Answer To Say

- “I initially proposed a dedicated Activity experience for agent plans, approvals, execution, and outcomes.”
- “The PM pushed back because Control Hub already had an audit log and a second system would create competing sources of truth.”
- “I agreed with that platform concern, but the existing events did not contain enough detail for consequential agent actions.”
- “I worked with engineering to define richer agent-event details inside the existing audit system.”
- “I changed the surface but protected the trust requirement. The result was a more coherent product direction.”

### If They Push

- **Was it shipped?** “No. This was directional product-definition work, so I describe the result as a stronger aligned direction, not a customer outcome.”
- **How do you handle disagreement?** “I try to disagree by reframing the user problem, not by arguing taste.”

## Primary Story 5: One-Report Technical Constraint

**Best for:** engineering partnership, technical limits, minimum viable scope, tradeoffs, and trustworthy AI.

**First-principles point:** The interface should never imply that an AI system understands more data than it actually receives.

### STAR

- **Situation:** The product direction was for CHAI to help administrators analyze reports and dashboards, but the first implementation could reliably pass only one report as context.
- **Task:** Deliver useful report analysis without creating a false impression of broad page-level understanding.
- **Action:** Placed the AI entry point directly on each report row. Opening it attached that specific report as context and made the scope visible. Designed the interaction pattern so it could later expand to page-level and dashboard analysis.
- **Result:** The first version stayed useful and technically honest while establishing a scalable contextual-AI pattern.
- **Reflection:** Reducing scope is good product judgment when the smaller version preserves the user contract and supports a credible path forward.

### Answer To Say

- “CHAI was intended to analyze reports, but the first technical version could reliably use only one report at a time.”
- “A page-level entry point would have implied that it understood everything on the page.”
- “I placed the entry point directly on the report row so the selected report and data scope were explicit.”
- “That gave us a useful first version without overstating the system's capability.”
- “As the technology improved, the same contract could expand to broader report and dashboard analysis.”

### If They Push

- **What did engineering contribute?** “Engineering clarified the reliable context boundary. I translated that boundary into an interaction the user could understand.”
- **Would you show a confidence score?** “Not by itself. Clear scope, supporting evidence, correction, and the authoritative report are more useful than a single abstract score.”

## Primary Story 6: SAP Navigation Failure

**Best for:** failure, accountability, difficult learning, user mental models, and changed behavior.

**First-principles point:** A validated component does not validate the larger behavior change around it.

### STAR

- **Situation:** At SAP, the product was moving toward more dashboard-based experiences, and the application navigation changed from horizontal to vertical.
- **Task:** Create a scalable navigation model using an established SAP design-system component.
- **Action:** Reused the component but moved too quickly. Did not study the existing user mental model deeply enough or test how people would locate familiar features after the change.
- **Result:** After launch, enterprise users gave strong negative feedback because they could no longer find things they relied on. The failure changed how major information-architecture decisions are approached.
- **Reflection:** For large behavior changes, design-system consistency cannot replace mental-model research, realistic task testing, and transition planning.

### Answer To Say

- “A real failure for me was a navigation redesign at SAP.”
- “I reused an established vertical-navigation component and assumed that made the direction safe.”
- “I did not research the existing user mental model deeply enough, and after launch users said they could no longer find important features.”
- “The mistake was mine: I treated a validated component as proof that the larger behavior change was also valid.”
- “It changed my process. I now test major navigation and workflow changes with realistic tasks and plan how existing users will transition.”

### If They Push

- **What would you do differently?** “Study current navigation behavior, use mental-model research and card sorting, test representative tasks, and introduce the change more deliberately.”
- **Why use this story?** It is a genuine failure with clear personal accountability. Do not soften it into a success story.

## Backup Story 7: Learning Troubleshooting With Technical Experts

**Best for:** learning a new domain, technical curiosity, research partnership, and evidence design.

**First-principles point:** To design for experts, learn how they reach a decision, not only which screens they currently use.

### STAR

- **Situation:** Control Hub troubleshooting required administrators to combine health data, meeting or calling details, device signals, logs, and support knowledge.
- **Task:** Understand how experienced support specialists formed a diagnosis before defining the AI experience.
- **Action:** Worked with a researcher and technical support experts to study real troubleshooting paths. Mapped affected scope, product area, signal patterns, issue type, required evidence, conflicting signals, and possible next actions. Used that model to structure the proposed interaction.
- **Result:** The concept moved from a chatbot giving a root-cause answer toward a reviewable diagnostic case with evidence, assumptions, gaps, and next steps.
- **Reflection:** Learning a domain means understanding its decision logic well enough to organize the evidence without pretending to replace the expert.

### Answer To Say

- “When I enter a new technical domain, I start with the decisions experts are making, not the terminology.”
- “For Control Hub troubleshooting, I worked with a researcher and technical support experts to study how they combined different signals into a diagnosis.”
- “I mapped the affected scope, evidence, conflicting signals, assumptions, and possible next actions.”
- “Then I explained the model back to the experts and used their corrections to refine it.”
- “That changed the concept from a chatbot giving an answer into a system that helped the administrator build and inspect a diagnostic case.”

### If They Push

- **How do you know you understand the domain?** “When I can explain the decision model clearly, predict the expert's next question, and identify where I still need their judgment.”
- **Status boundary:** Present the diagnostic interaction and shareable case as directional unless later shipping or validation is confirmed.

## Backup Story 8: SAP Role-Based Homepage

**Best for:** initiative, going beyond scope, scalable systems, role-based design, and measurable reach.

**First-principles point:** Technical modernization creates more value when it also improves the default experience for the people using it.

### STAR

- **Situation:** The project began as rebuilding homepage components with SAP's UI5 framework.
- **Task:** Ensure the new component system supported the real work of different user roles rather than becoming only a technical migration.
- **Action:** Studied the responsibilities and information needs of four personas. Proposed role-based dashboard templates using the new reusable components, with relevant work items, signals, and actions for each role.
- **Result:** The project expanded from component modernization into a stronger role-based product experience. The modular homepage framework was adopted by more than 1,000 enterprise customers.
- **Reflection:** The best systems work connects reuse for the team with a clearer default experience for the customer.

### Answer To Say

- “At SAP, the original homepage project focused on rebuilding UI components with the UI5 framework.”
- “While working on it, I found that different roles needed very different information and actions from the homepage.”
- “I researched four personas and proposed role-based dashboard templates built from the same reusable components.”
- “That expanded the project from a component migration into a better default experience for each role.”
- “The modular framework was eventually adopted by more than 1,000 enterprise customers.”

### If They Push

- **What was your contribution?** “I identified the role-based opportunity, framed the model, and turned it into a product direction. Delivery remained cross-functional.”

## Backup Story 9: Control Hub Should Still Feel Like Control Hub

**Best for:** difficult feedback, changing direction, product taste, restraint, and avoiding AI theater.

**First-principles point:** Good AI design should add capability without forcing users to abandon a product model they already trust.

### STAR

- **Situation:** An early Agentic direction gave AI too much visual and structural gravity inside Control Hub.
- **Task:** Make the new capability understandable without allowing AI to replace the familiar operational product.
- **Action:** Accepted feedback that administrators still needed a recognizable Control Hub home. Changed the model so conversation handled open-ended intent while plans, workflows, execution, and history lived in durable product surfaces.
- **Result:** The directional concept became more coherent, more useful, and less theatrical.
- **Reflection:** Making AI prominent is not the same as making AI valuable.

### Answer To Say

- “The most useful feedback on the Agentic direction was that it could not feel like AI was replacing Control Hub.”
- “My early concept gave the AI surface too much gravity because I was trying to make the new strategy visible.”
- “The feedback reminded me that administrators still needed a familiar operational home.”
- “I changed the model so chat handled intent, while plans, execution, and Activity lived in recognizable product structures.”
- “The result was less theatrical and more trustworthy. The lesson was that new capability should not erase the user's existing mental model.”

### If They Push

- **Was this validated or shipped?** “It was directional feedback and product-definition work, not a shipped outcome.”

## Delivery Rules

- Start with your answer. Do not spend the first 30 seconds explaining the company or project.
- Keep the situation to one or two sentences.
- Spend most of the answer on your decisions and why you made them.
- Say “I” for your contribution and “we” for the team's delivery and outcome.
- Name partners without blaming them.
- State whether work was shipped, measured, directional, or prototyped.
- Use one result. Do not stack every metric into every answer.
- End with the principle you learned or now use.
- Pause after the result. Let Jean choose the follow-up.

## Avoid These Phrases

- “I followed the design process.”
- “I simplified a complex experience.”
- “We collaborated closely.”
- “I made the AI feel magical.”
- “The model will figure it out.”
- “Engineering said no.”
- “The users loved it.”
- “I was responsible for everything.”

Replace them with specific decisions:

- “I made the data scope visible because the system could use only one report.”
- “I changed the model from technical context to user-facing Skills.”
- “I preserved the audit log but added the detail agent actions required.”
- “I built the execution and failure states because static screens hid the risk.”
