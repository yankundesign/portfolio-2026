## The Company In Plain English

Annora is building a private AI operating system for machinery and equipment manufacturers. It starts with sales quoting and customer service, connects to the ERP, CRM, spreadsheets, drawings, files, and email that a company already uses, and adds department-specific workspaces on top. The company positions the product as an incremental layer rather than a rip-and-replace ERP transition. [Product](https://annora.ai/product/) · [FAQ](https://annora.ai/faq/) · [Manifesto](https://annora.ai/manifesto/)

The simple workflow model:

1. A customer inquiry arrives with requirements, attachments, and incomplete language.
2. Annora finds relevant product knowledge, previous quotes, rules, pricing, and specifications across existing systems.
3. ANI proposes a configuration and helps the employee modify it.
4. The employee reviews selections, price, wording, terms, and supporting records.
5. The employee approves and sends the finished quote.
6. The same company knowledge later supports service, procurement, field reporting, and other workflows.

The value proposition is not "chat with factory data." It is:

- Preserve operational knowledge that is scattered across systems and experienced employees.
- Reduce the time and specialist effort required to quote complex equipment.
- Avoid duplicate entry and errors across disconnected tools.
- Add automation without forcing a risky multi-year ERP replacement.
- Keep company and customer IP private.
- Turn each deployment into a reusable foundation for more workflows.

## What Annora Is Really Hiring This Designer To Change

### Role thesis

Annora needs a first-principles design owner who can turn high-touch, manufacturer-specific workflows into clear product UI and then encode the successful decisions as a reusable, machine-checkable system.

### Product pain points

- Quoting combines customer language, configuration rules, price, historical precedent, technical feasibility, and approvals.
- Source data is fragmented, inconsistent, outdated, or trapped in employee memory.
- Different customers will share workflow patterns but use different terminology, systems, rules, and exceptions.
- AI needs to be useful without obscuring evidence, assumptions, or decision boundaries.
- Employees may have decades of domain expertise but little patience for a new software model.
- A bad quote can create downstream engineering and production problems, not just a confusing screen.
- Product UI, customer workspaces, marketing pages, and implementation artifacts all need a coherent quality bar.

### Team pain points

- The founders and engineers need one person to own product hierarchy, interaction behavior, and visual quality end to end.
- Design decisions need to survive beyond a Figma file.
- Repeated customer work must become reusable patterns rather than one-off UI.
- The designer must explain why a pattern works in plain language and encode it as rules that tools and code can validate.
- The team likely cannot support a designer who waits for perfectly defined requirements or hands off static screens.

### Business outcome

- Shorten the path from inquiry to an accurate, approved quote.
- Help customers see value quickly enough to expand to additional workspaces.
- Make a custom-feeling product deployable without custom-designing everything from zero.
- Raise customer trust and adoption.
- Increase the team's product and implementation velocity without lowering visual quality.

- **Breadth:** Product UI, customer-facing web design, and design systems.
