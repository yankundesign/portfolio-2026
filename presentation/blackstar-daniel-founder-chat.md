# Blackstar — 20-Minute Founder Chat with Daniel Edrisian

## What I want Daniel to remember

- I turn complicated systems into clear product behavior.
- I think beyond screens: context, intent, action, control, and recovery.
- I can move between product strategy, interaction details, and working prototypes.
- I want early ownership and direct collaboration, not a narrower design lane.

## My point of view

Today's computers make people translate a goal into a sequence of apps, menus, and commands.

AI can reduce that translation work, but only if the system:

- Understands the person's context.
- Shows what it understood.
- Makes its intended action clear.
- Keeps the person in control when the consequence matters.

That is why Blackstar is interesting. The opportunity is not to add chat to another device. It is to reconsider the relationship between a person and a computer at the system level.

The core design tensions are:

- Intelligent without becoming invisible.
- Proactive without becoming intrusive.
- Personal without becoming invasive.
- Powerful without taking away agency.

Ask two or three questions. Follow his answers instead of reading a checklist.

### 18–20 minutes — Process and close

State interest clearly and ask about the next step.

## Why Blackstar

- **Q: Why are you interested in Blackstar?**
  - **Testing:** Am I interested in the actual problem or just the AI category?
  - **Answer to say:**
    - “What interests me is that you’re not treating AI as another feature inside an existing application.”
    - “People still have to translate a goal into a sequence of tools and commands.”
    - “AI can reduce that work, but only if the system understands context and keeps the person in control.”
    - “Blackstar is approaching that problem through hardware, software, and interaction together.”
    - “That is a rare, foundational design problem, and it’s the kind of work I want to be closer to.”

## How I think about an AI-native computer

- **Q: What should an AI-native computer be?**
  - **Testing:** Can I form a clear product point of view without guessing the product?
  - **Answer to say:**
    - “I don’t think it means putting chat on every surface.”
    - “A conventional computer makes the person remember where information lives and how each tool works.”
    - “An AI-native computer should understand the person’s current context and help move the goal forward.”
    - “But it must also show what it knows, what it plans to do, and when the person still needs to decide.”
    - “The hardest problem is not capability alone. It is balancing intelligence with agency, attention, and trust.”

## How I would start from zero

- **Q: How would you begin designing something this new?**
  - **Testing:** Can I create clarity without waiting for a complete brief?
  - **Answer to say:**
    - “I would start with the first user and the recurring job the device can improve dramatically.”
    - “Then I’d identify where today’s computing model creates unnecessary translation, fragmentation, or attention cost.”
    - “I’d prototype a few complete moments, including what the system perceives, how it communicates its understanding, when it acts, and how the person corrects it.”
    - “I’d bring engineering constraints into those prototypes early.”
    - “The goal would be to test the riskiest assumption before building a broad product around it.”

## My strongest relevant story

- **Q: What work have you done that is most relevant?**
  - **Testing:** Have I solved a related problem with real evidence?
  - **Answer to say:**
    - “The closest shipped example is the AI assistant I work on in Cisco Control Hub.”
    - “Administrators needed to understand complicated network data, but a separate chat destination removed them from the dashboard containing the real context.”
    - “We brought AI into the existing workflow so it could help interpret reports and troubleshoot issues around the information they were already viewing.”
    - “Across the broader CHAI experience, monthly adoption grew from 3% to 18%.”
    - “The main lesson was that intelligence becomes useful when it understands where the person is and preserves the authoritative context around the answer.”

## How I work with ambiguity

- **Q: How do you move quickly when the problem is unclear?**
  - **Testing:** Will I create momentum or wait for direction?
  - **Answer to say:**
    - “I first separate what we know from what we are assuming.”
    - “Then I identify the decision we need to make and the cheapest evidence that would improve it.”
    - “I make the idea tangible enough for people to react to, usually through a prototype.”
    - “I don’t wait for complete certainty, but I make the tradeoff and remaining risk explicit.”

## Questions for Daniel

### How to use this list

- Ask two or three questions, not all of them.
- Start with vision, then move to the product or role, and end with culture.
- Choose a question Daniel has not already answered.
- Use short follow-ups. A real conversation leaves a better impression than a perfect list.

### Best three for this 20-minute conversation

#### 1. Understand the ambition

“If Blackstar succeeds, what will people no longer have to do when they use a computer?”

**What this reveals:** The human change behind the technology, in Daniel’s own words.

#### 2. Understand the product

“What has the prototype taught you that you did not expect when you started?”

**What this reveals:** What is real today, how the team learns, and where the vision has changed.

#### 3. Understand the culture

“Because the product crosses hardware, software, and design, how do you want the team to make decisions when those disciplines pull in different directions?”

**What this reveals:** How Daniel leads, how disagreement works, and whether design has a real voice.

### Vision and founding insight

- “What did you see that convinced you this required a new device and operating system, rather than better software on existing computers?”
- “What part of today’s relationship with computers feels most broken to you?”
- “What do you believe about the future of computing that most people in the industry have not recognized yet?”
- “If we look five years ahead and Blackstar has worked, what will feel fundamentally different in everyday life?”
- “What about this problem felt important enough for you to leave Codex and build a new company around it?”

### Product and interaction

- “Who is the first person you want to make this for, and what is the first moment that needs to feel meaningfully better than a phone or laptop?”
- “Without getting into anything confidential, what can the prototype already do today?”
- “Which interaction problem still feels the most unresolved?”
- “How do you think about what the computer should remember, when it should act on its own, and when it should ask?”
- “Where do you think current AI products ask too much of the user?”
- “What is the biggest product assumption the team still needs to prove?”
- “What would make someone use this as their computer every day, rather than as an interesting second device?”

### Design ambition and ownership

- “Why is now the right time to bring in a product designer?”
- “What would you want the designer to own in the first three months?”
- “Six months in, what would an exceptional designer have changed for the product or the team?”
- “Which decisions are still open enough for this person to shape from first principles?”
- “What do you hope a strong designer will challenge you or the team on?”
- “How do you want product design and industrial design to influence each other?”

### Team and culture

- “What kind of person does their best work at Blackstar?”
- “What behaviors help someone earn trust quickly on this team?”
- “How do you balance moving fast with protecting the quality of something people will use every day?”
- “When the team strongly disagrees, how do you like to reach a decision?”
- “What do you want the culture to feel like while the team is still small?”
- “What have you learned from building one team across San Francisco and Shenzhen?”
- “What part of the company are you personally staying closest to as the team grows?”

### Natural follow-ups

Use these when Daniel says something interesting:
- “What led you to that conclusion?”
- “Can you give me an example?”
- “What surprised you about that?”
- “Has your thinking changed since you started?”
- “What makes that difficult in practice?”
- “How are you testing that today?”

### Recommended closing sequence

If there are about five minutes left:
1. “If Blackstar succeeds, what will people no longer have to do when they use a computer?”
2. “What has the prototype taught you that you did not expect?”
3. “Six months in, what would an exceptional designer have changed?”

If Daniel has already explained the vision, replace the first question with the culture question about how hardware, software, and design make decisions together.

## Avoid

- Do not describe Blackstar as a chatbot for the whole computer.
- Do not guess the device or speak as if I know their confidential product.
- Do not repeat “AI-native” without explaining the user problem.
- Do not spend several minutes explaining Cisco’s organization.
- Do not present Agentic work as shipped.
- Do not apologize repeatedly for lacking hardware experience.
- Do not ask every prepared question. Listen and follow the interesting thread.
- Do not say “software is solved” as an absolute claim. Explain the shift in the user–computer relationship instead.
