A · Specimen cover

Slide 1 of the deck, made into the desk.

  1. The name becomes the hero: display serif, “Wang.” in italic, set on specimen guides (cap height, x-height, baseline).
  2. Those guides replace the scattered readouts (format, date, columns, gutter, margin, column numbers). Construction lines stay visible, in one place.
  3. One hand-drawn baseline carries everything: the name, roulette, notebook and polaroid all rest on it.
  4. Cobalt enters as the single accent, used once: the cube.
  5. Ephemera (sketch, stamp, blueprint) leave. Four objects remain.
  6. Captions become liner notes: mono label over an italic serif line.
  7. Flat warm paper. The cool desk-light vignette goes.

Trade-off: the strongest identity moment of the three, and the closest match to the deck. The desk gets smaller; the notebook stays the largest target.

B · Thesis first

Slides 3 and 4 of the deck, stacked.

  1. The through-line leads: “I make complex, invisible systems handleable.” Same treatment as the deck: “invisible” greyed, “handleable.” in cobalt italic at display size.
  2. The name drops to a masthead with the cube.
  3. The deck’s path timeline (architecture → coffee shop → HCI → SAP → Cisco) becomes the about-in-one-line.
  4. Objects gather into a right-hand pile on the same hand-drawn baseline.

Trade-off: the fastest read for a founder skimming. The desk metaphor becomes secondary, and cobalt shows up twice on one screen.

C · Restyle in place

The live composition, with the deck’s type and colour.

  1. Same objects, same positions, same plate frame.
  2. The masthead moves to the deck display face at twice the size, with the cube.
  3. Chrome cut to two mono labels. Column numbers and readouts go; faint column rulings stay.
  4. The thesis replaces the bottom-left readout, with “handleable.” in cobalt.
  5. Liner-note captions, a hand-drawn baseline under the object row, flat paper.

Trade-off: the lowest-risk option, and it keeps the signature desk-to-notebook transition untouched. The least change in first impression.
