Card hover — three approaches

Field notes · prototype · 2026-05-04
Option 01 · the obvious one

Lift-and-Loft

Hover scales the plate ~1.5× and tilts it 2–3° toward the spread's center. Neighboring plates dim to 0.4 to clear the eye. The plate hinges from a low pivot so the gesture reads as paper being lifted off the page, not a flat zoom.

Cheapest to ship. Extends the existing tilt-on-hover vocabulary instead of inventing a new gesture. The risk: it doesn't add a new beat to the site's craft inventory — it's the safe answer.

Option 02 · the editorial one

Specimen drawer

The card on the page stays put. On hover, a deeper drawer slides out from beneath carrying a full-size plate, the museum label, the impact line, and a bit of body copy. Like a flat-file drawer in a print archive being pulled open.

Editorially correct — the canvas becomes a collection, not a gallery. Costs more layout work: each drawer needs row space to slide into, which forces decisions about how the page reflows.

Option 03 · the field-notebook one

Loupe

A magnifying loupe — small ink-stroked circle, ~160px — follows the cursor when hovering inside a plate. Within the loupe the plate renders at 2×; outside, at rest size. The cursor effectively becomes a tool, like inspecting a print under a 10× lens.

The most field-notebook gesture on the list. The interaction is the metaphor. Risks: gimmicky if the lens doesn't feel like a real tool, and the user only sees a window into the plate, never the whole thing.
