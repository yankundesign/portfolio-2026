# Voice & Writing Rules

All portfolio copy follows these rules from the vibe-guideline.

## Tone

First person, reflective, cutting. Fraunces is doing typographic work — the prose must match that weight, with edge. Every paragraph earns one sharp observation, decision, or change of direction. Lingering is the enemy.

The register is founding-designer, not mid-stage-polish. Less "here's what I noticed and pondered." More "here's what I killed and why."

## Do

- Write in first person: "I spent three months in Control Hub before proposing anything."
- Lead case studies with a specific moment or observation, not a problem statement
- Credit collaborators by role, not by name. "The PM and I went back and forth on this for a week." Collaborators are never named inline or in credits — this is a site-wide rule.
- Share what didn't work before what did — shows confidence
- Keep sentences short when the thought is simple; let one longer sentence do real work per paragraph
- Make every paragraph cut, reframe, or advance the thought. If it only lingers, kill it.
- Trust specific verbs. "I cut," "I shipped," "I argued" beat "I explored," "I considered," "I worked on."
- Name what you changed your mind about mid-project. Reversals are evidence of thinking.

## Don't

- No "My Role · My Team · My Timeline" résumé boxes — put metadata inline or in credits
- No hero problem statements in large type — let the work speak
- No empty confidence words: "robust," "seamless," "user-centric," "innovative"
- No passive voice for own decisions: say "I cut the welcome modal in half" not "the redesign increased adoption"
- No LinkedIn prose, no manifesto tone
- No decorative sentences. A beautiful sentence that doesn't do work is a dead sentence.
- No hedging: "it seemed like," "perhaps," "arguably." State the call or don't make it.

## Length guidance

| Element | Length |
|---|---|
| Landing tagline | 10–16 words, one thought |
| Project summary | 25–40 words |
| Case study essay | 400–800 words |
| Journal page | 200–400 words |
| Marginalia | 2–6 words max |
| Colophon essay | 300–500 words |

## Important precision rule

When describing the CHAI work: Yankun built an AI assistant to help users interpret existing dashboards — not the dashboards themselves. Don't let summaries drift.
